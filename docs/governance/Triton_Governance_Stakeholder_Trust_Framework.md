# Card 1 — Stakeholder Trust & Legitimacy Philosophy

### Purpose of institutional trust

Stakeholder trust is the **credibility others place in Triton’s governance**—that decisions are contained, evidenced, overseen, and honestly reported. Legitimacy is earned by **discipline over time**, not by performance narratives or quiet quarters.

### Why governance legitimacy must be earned and continuously preserved

| Erosion pattern | Preservation discipline |
|-----------------|-------------------------|
| Hide Critical from oversight | 96 executive line; 90 severity truth |
| LP deck ≠ audit pack | 107 single evidence index |
| Crisis spin | 108 contain-first; Card 6 prudence |
| “Institutional grade” marketing | 110 earned cert; Card 8 anti-hype |
| Opacity during diligence | 107 Q&A traceability |
| Ethics breach becomes reputation event | 116 → `GOVTRUST` linkage |

### Core principles

| Principle | Trust meaning |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Capital Preservation Doctrine supremacy** | Credibility follows containment, not bravado |
| **Integrity before reputation** | Fix truth before managing perception (116) |
| **Trust through evidence** | Artifacts, not adjectives |
| **Constitutional safeguards dominate** | Legitimacy never buys safeguard waivers |
| **Truth before convenience** | No under-reporting severity |
| **Transparency with prudence** | Card 6 balance—disclose what duty requires |
| **Institutional accountability** | Named owners in 96, 106 |
| **Credibility through discipline** | Readiness and maturity earned (110, 114) |

### What institutional trust proves

- Stakeholder groups have **defined trust expectations** (Card 2)
- Trust risks are **classified and escalated** (Card 3)
- Communication aligns with **evidence** (96, 107)—not speculation
- Breaches trigger **containment and recovery** playbooks (Cards 5, 7)
- Hype and overstatement are **actively resisted** (Card 8)

### What institutional trust cannot guarantee

- Positive press or social sentiment
- LP retention or fundraising success
- Regulatory approval or zero examination findings
- Immunity from rumor or misinformation
- Market performance
- That all stakeholders will always believe narratives

# Card 6 — Transparency, Communication & Prudence Model

| Balance | Why delicate | Failure consequence | Escalation | Corrective expectation |
|---------|--------------|---------------------|------------|------------------------|
| **Transparency vs confidentiality** | LP right to know vs leak risk | Over/under share | Committee defines audience | Tiered disclosure matrix |
| **Accountability vs overexposure** | Blame vs learning | Toxic culture | Lead | System fix, named owners |
| **Trust vs speculation** | Stakeholders want certainty | False promises | Executive restraint | “We do not know yet” allowed |
| **Evidence vs narrative distortion** | Story easier than logs | Credibility gap | 96 correction | Attach artifacts to updates |
| **Calm communication under pressure** | Panic spreads | Rumor amplification | Single voice (108) | Scheduled factual updates |
| **Crisis communication discipline** | Hurry to reassure | Premature lift narrative | Hard Halt until vote | Contain-first template |
| **Legitimacy without hype** | Desire to impress | Overclaim | Card 8 | Under-promise, over-deliver evidence |

**Prudence rule:** Disclose **what oversight requires** and **what diligence requests**—never trade secrets, never forward performance guarantees, never imply runtime authorization from governance posture.
